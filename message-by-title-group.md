# Message by title group: imaging center cold email

Source: the Alpha Nodus Message House (§2.5 personas, approved lines) and the imaging-industry primer, from `Alpha_nodus-0.20.2-v49.zip`.

**Status key**
- **APPROVED**: quoted verbatim from the Message House.
- **DRAFT**: written by Claude from approved lines. The Message House has no row for this title group, so it needs the marketing lead or Shamit's sign-off before it goes out.

**Rules for every email**
- Say "can", never "will". Quote only the modeled figures that are approved.
- Never say Gravity replaces the RIS. Gravity is the system that works on top of your CRM, RIS and RCM.
- No "SOC 2 certified" or "HIPAA certified". The approved wording is "HIPAA compliant, with a BAA with every customer."
- Never state a net benefit, ROI multiple or payback. We state the gross benefit and the buyer subtracts the price.
- No "cut staff" or "sidekick". Staff are never the problem.
- Clinical work (the scan, the read) is never Gravity's work.
- The master promise: no referral, order or revenue leaks. The job: get the order, complete the exam, get paid.

| Send first | Send second | Don't cold-email as primary targets |
|---|---|---|
| Owner / founder (1), C-suite (2), Administrator (3), Billing / finance (10) | Director / VP (4), Marketing / BD (9), IT / PACS (12) | Radiologist (5), Other physician (6), Nurse / PA (7), Front desk (8), Technologist (11), Other (13). They influence the decision but rarely buy. Reach them through the buyer, or with a short, softer note. |

## 1. Owner / founder / managing partner
- **Maps to:** Owner / CEO persona.
- **What they do:** Run the business. Often a founding physician or a private-equity-backed management team. They own the P&L and make the final call.
- **What they focus on:** Exam volume, utilization, margin, growth without hiring.
- **Pain, in their words:** "After 20 years you get used to your shortcomings and you hire around them." "I have 12 people doing faxes and auth."
- **Leak they feel:** All three.
- **Real objection:** "Is this real, at our size?"
- **Subject (DRAFT):** Where the money leaks between referral and payment
- **Message (APPROVED lines, DRAFT arrangement):**
  > Imaging centers don't lose money in the scanner. They lose it between the referral and the payment. Gravity can capture more referrals, fill more slots and collect more of what you bill, without adding headcount. A typical imaging center makes $216,000 a year on $4.4 million. With Gravity, it can make $585,000, before Gravity's price. Worth 20 minutes?
- **Avoid:** Anything you can't prove. Never a promise ("will") about their own center.

## 2. C-suite / president / CEO
- **Maps to:** Owner / CEO. If the title is COO or CFO, use that persona's row instead (see 3 and 10).
- **What they do:** Set direction, own growth and margin, answer to the owners or board.
- **What they focus on:** Growth, margin, vendor count, risk, and whether a new system adds work or removes it.
- **Leak they feel:** All three.
- **Real objection:** "Is this real, at our size?"
- **Subject (DRAFT):** $5 of every $100
- **Message (DRAFT, figures APPROVED):**
  > A typical imaging center keeps $5 of every $100 it collects. $26 of every $100 goes to the work between the referral and the payment: the faxes, the booking, the eligibility checks, the authorizations and the claims. That is the work Gravity is built to do. No referral, order or revenue leaks, in one system.
- **Avoid:** Same as Owner. Lead with the margin figure, not the technology.

## 3. Administrator / practice or center manager
- **Maps to:** COO / operations director (inferred: the primer calls them the person who "runs the imaging center day-to-day" and a "typical buyer"). Not in the Message House by name.
- **What they do:** Run the center day to day: staffing, scheduling, the front desk, referrals coming in, billing hand-offs, patient complaints.
- **What they focus on:** Hold time, abandonment, no-shows, backlog, fax volume, re-keying, staff turnover.
- **Pain, in their words:** "We trip over ourselves. It exhausts us." "Six to seven thousand faxes a month, four pages each."
- **Leak they feel:** The order.
- **Real objection:** "What happens to my team?"
- **Subject (DRAFT):** Nothing sits in a queue
- **Message (APPROVED lines, DRAFT arrangement):**
  > Nothing sits in a queue. Every order becomes an exam. Gravity means fewer no-shows, shorter phone queues and no re-keying: the work runs on its own from the fax to check-in. Gravity takes over the tasks, not the people. The routine work completes on its own, so your team can give patients the attention the paperwork used to take.
- **Avoid:** Anything that makes their staff the problem. "Sidekick."
- **If they are effectively the owner** (small center): use the Owner message.

## 4. Director / VP / head
- **Maps to:** Depends on the function after "Director of": Operations → COO. Revenue cycle / billing → CFO / revenue cycle. Marketing / outreach → Marketing lead. IT → CIO. Default to COO.
- **What they do:** Run one function and answer to the C-suite. They often write the shortlist and run the evaluation.
- **What they focus on:** Their function's numbers, and being able to show the C-suite a result.
- **Subject (DRAFT):** Pick the function's own approved line (e.g., "Authorized before arrival" for revenue cycle).
- **Message:** Use the persona message for their function (see 3, 9, 10, 12). Add one line: "Happy to send a one-page version you can forward."
- **Avoid:** Using a generic line. Check their actual function from the title first.

## 5. Radiologist / imaging physician
- **Maps to:** Medical director rule: CEO row plus one line. Not a persona on its own.
- **What they do:** Read the studies and sign the report. Often an owner or partner in the practice.
- **What they focus on:** Turnaround on reads, getting complete orders and clinical information, report quality, the referrers who send them work.
- **Leak they feel:** Order (incomplete orders) and Referral (reports reaching the referring office).
- **Subject (DRAFT):** Nobody waits for care because of paperwork
- **Message (DRAFT, first line APPROVED):**
  > Nobody waits for care because of paperwork. Gravity works on the orders, the authorizations and the report back to the referring office, which can reach them the day it's signed. The read stays yours: Gravity does none of the clinical work.
- **Avoid:** Any suggestion Gravity touches the read or diagnosis. Clinical work is never Gravity's work.

## 6. Other physician / surgeon / partner
- **Maps to:** Either a partner/owner in the practice (use Owner) or a referring physician (not a buyer). Check which from the context before sending.
- **What they do:** Order imaging for patients, or own part of the practice.
- **What they focus on:** Getting patients scanned quickly, getting results back, not chasing the center.
- **Subject (DRAFT):** The report back, the day it's signed
- **Message (DRAFT, uses an APPROVED capability line):**
  > When your patient is scanned, you can hear back the day the report is signed, with referral tracking behind it. If you are a partner in the practice, the thing I'd show you is how the work between the referral and the payment can run on its own.
- **Avoid:** Treating a referring physician as a buyer. Don't pitch software to them.

## 7. Nurse / PA / clinical
- **Maps to:** No persona. Closest is the COO row's staff concern.
- **What they do:** Prepare patients, handle screening, contrast and prior-auth questions, call patients back.
- **What they focus on:** Patients arriving prepared and authorized, fewer phone-tag loops, less paperwork between them and patient care.
- **Subject (DRAFT):** Less paperwork between you and the patient
- **Message (DRAFT, team answer APPROVED):**
  > Gravity takes over the tasks, not the people. The routine work completes on its own, so your team can give patients the attention the paperwork used to take.
- **Avoid:** Anything about replacing staff or clinical judgment.

## 8. Scheduling / front desk / admin staff
- **Maps to:** Booking lead.
- **What they do:** Answer phones, book exams, key in orders from faxes, check patients in, chase missing information.
- **What they focus on:** Phone volume, no-shows, re-keying, a full schedule at the end of the day.
- **Leak they feel:** Order.
- **Subject (DRAFT):** Review the work instead of re-keying it
- **Message (APPROVED lines):**
  > Your team reviews the work instead of re-keying it. Patients book by text or by voice, no-shows are refilled on their own, and the phones quiet down. Gravity takes over the tasks, not the people.
- **Avoid:** Anything that makes them feel replaced. They are the day-to-day users, so write it as relief.

## 9. Marketing / sales / business development
- **Maps to:** Marketing / outreach lead.
- **What they do:** Manage referring-physician relationships through liaisons or reps, track referral volume, run outreach.
- **What they focus on:** Orders by referrer, new versus lost referrers, liaison productivity.
- **Pain, in their words:** "We don't have the ability to measure leakage."
- **Leak they feel:** Referral.
- **Real objection:** "We already have a CRM and liaisons."
- **Subject (DRAFT):** Who stopped referring?
- **Message (APPROVED lines):**
  > Know who refers, who stopped, and win them back. Referral tracking and referrer outreach for physician liaisons, with the report back to the office the day it's signed. It works on top of your CRM.
- **Avoid:** "Replaces your reps."

## 10. Billing / finance / compliance
- **Maps to:** CFO / revenue cycle (billing, finance). For compliance, add the security statements.
- **What they do:** Verify insurance, get prior authorizations, submit claims, work denials and A/R.
- **What they focus on:** Denial rate, clean-claim rate, days in A/R, cost to collect, cost per exam.
- **Pain, in their words:** "We're billing the wrong insurance because they don't inactivate insurances."
- **Leak they feel:** Revenue.
- **Real objection:** "Show me the ROI."
- **Subject (DRAFT):** Authorized before arrival
- **Message (APPROVED lines):**
  > Every exam you complete, you get paid for. Authorized before arrival: the denial is fixed before it exists. A denial costs more than an empty slot. Both lose the payment, but for a denial you have already paid for the read: $22.50 an exam in a typical center.
- **For compliance:** Add "HIPAA compliant, with a BAA with every customer."
- **Avoid:** "Drives efficiency," "RPA bots," a net benefit or ROI multiple we calculate.

## 11. Technologist / sonographer
- **Maps to:** No persona. Not a buyer.
- **What they do:** Perform the exam, position the patient, run the equipment. Clinical work.
- **What they focus on:** On-time schedule, patients who arrive ready, correct orders, no waiting on missing information.
- **Subject (DRAFT):** Patients who arrive ready
- **Message (DRAFT):**
  > Gravity settles the order, the authorization and the booking before the patient arrives, so the exam starts on time with the right paperwork. The scan stays yours: Gravity does none of the clinical work.
- **Avoid:** Anything about the exam itself. Don't cold-email as a primary target; use as an internal champion.

## 12. IT / informatics / PACS
- **Maps to:** CIO / IT and security. The named blocker.
- **What they do:** Run the RIS, PACS, interfaces and security. Gatekeep new vendors.
- **What they focus on:** Vendor count, interfaces, PHI exposure, uptime, SSO, audit trail, data ownership.
- **Pain, in their words:** "They host it. They don't let me in."
- **Real objection:** "Is cloud safe for PHI? Will you break my interfaces?"
- **Subject (DRAFT):** One cloud system on top of your RIS and PACS
- **Message (APPROVED lines):**
  > One cloud system for the work, on top of the systems you already run. It can run on top of your RIS, PACS and RCM, over HL7, FHIR and the browser where no interface exists. AWS US regions only. HIPAA compliant, with a BAA with every customer. Independently penetration-tested every year.
- **Avoid:** "More secure," "SOC 2 certified," "HIPAA certified."

## 13. Other or no title
- **Maps to:** No persona. Use the master promise.
- **Message (DRAFT, lines APPROVED):**
  > No referral, order or revenue leaks. Get the order. Complete the exam. Get paid. Gravity does that work for imaging centers, on top of the RIS, CRM and RCM they already use. Who at your center owns this work?
- **Goal:** Find the right person, not to pitch.

## When to send (best days and times)

**Status: general cold-email benchmarks plus my reasoning about each role's workday. This is not Alpha Nodus data, and the Message House says nothing on send timing.** Treat it as a starting hypothesis and test it against your own open and reply rates (Metricool or Gmail history can supply those).

**Default for all groups:** Tuesday to Thursday, in the recipient's local time zone. Avoid Friday afternoon and weekends. Monday is the weakest weekday for most of these roles, because inboxes are full of weekend faxes and referrals. If you do send Monday, aim for 1:00 to 3:00 PM local, after the morning backlog, and expect lower replies than midweek.

| Title group | Best days | Best time (local) | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Owner / founder | Tue to Thu | 7:00 to 9:00 AM, or 4:00 to 6:00 PM | Owners read email before the day starts or after clinic and operations wind down. |
| 2. C-suite / CEO | Tue to Thu | 7:00 to 9:00 AM | Same pattern; short, early messages are most likely to be read. |
| 3. Administrator / center manager | Tue to Thu | 10:00 AM to 12:00 PM, or 1:30 to 3:30 PM | Busiest at opening and at the Monday fax backlog; mid-morning and early afternoon are calmer. |
| 4. Director / VP | Tue to Thu | 8:00 to 10:00 AM | Office hours; they check email early between meetings. |
| 5. Radiologist | Tue to Thu | 6:00 to 8:00 AM, or 12:00 to 1:00 PM | Reading volume peaks mid-morning and early afternoon; early or lunch-hour windows are free. |
| 6. Other physician / surgeon | Tue to Thu | 6:30 to 8:00 AM, or 12:00 to 1:00 PM | Before clinic or surgery, or at lunch. |
| 7. Nurse / PA / clinical | Tue to Thu | 12:00 to 1:00 PM, or 3:00 to 4:00 PM | Gaps between patients. Low priority anyway. |
| 8. Scheduling / front desk | Wed or Thu | 10:00 to 11:00 AM, or 2:00 to 3:00 PM | Phones peak at opening, lunch and close. Avoid Monday completely. |
| 9. Marketing / BD | Tue to Thu | 9:00 to 11:00 AM | Desk-based role with regular email habits; liaisons are often in the field, so early morning or end of day. |
| 10. Billing / finance / compliance | Tue to Thu | 9:00 to 11:00 AM, or 1:00 to 3:00 PM | Avoid the last two days and first two days of the month, when they close the books and push claims. |
| 11. Technologist / sonographer | Tue to Thu | 12:00 to 1:00 PM | Low priority; they are on the floor and rarely at a desk. |
| 12. IT / informatics / PACS | Tue to Thu | 9:00 to 11:00 AM | Desk-based; avoid Monday (tickets) and anything near a maintenance window. |
| 13. Other / no title | Tue to Thu | 10:00 AM | Default. |

**Practical rules**
- Send to one title group at a time per center, a few days apart, not the whole center at once.
- Follow up after 3 to 4 business days, then once more a week later. Change the angle each time (pain, then the modeled figure, then the ask).
- Use the recipient's time zone. Imaging centers span all US zones, so schedule by the center's zip code.
- Test: run Tuesday 8 AM against Thursday 1 PM for the Administrator and CFO groups for two weeks, and keep whichever gets the higher reply rate.
