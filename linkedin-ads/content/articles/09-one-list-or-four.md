# One list or four? Why work gets lost between the fax, the phone, the text and the portal

*For operations and practice managers*

Walk through your front office on a Monday morning and count the places work can arrive. There is the fax tray. There is the phone, with its voicemail. There are patient texts, referring-office emails, and whatever is waiting in a payer or referrer portal.

Each of those is a list, and each list has a different person checking it at a different time.

## The cost is not the minutes

It is tempting to measure this in minutes per fax or minutes per call. That misses the real problem. When work arrives in four places, nobody owns the whole picture. A call-back request sits in voicemail while the matching fax sits in a tray. The patient who replied to a text is waiting on someone who is busy with the phone.

Nothing is broken. Each person is doing their part. The gaps are between the lists.

## Four questions to ask

1. **What is the oldest item waiting right now, in any channel?** If you can't answer without walking around, that is the first finding.
2. **When a patient replies to a text, who sees it, and how fast?**
3. **When a fax is missing a detail, where is that tracked?** On a sticky note, in someone's head, or in one place everyone can see.
4. **When someone is out, who picks up their list?** If the answer is "whoever notices", work will wait.

## One list changes what you can see

The fix is not more people watching more lists. It is one list that every channel feeds, with each item assigned to a person as it arrives.

In Gravity Work, every call, text, email, document, order, referring provider and patient item goes onto a worklist. Work is assigned the moment it arrives, up to each person's capacity and skill, so the person who gets a task is able to do it. Search works by meaning, including handwriting on a fax. Gravity Communication keeps every conversation, whichever channel it came through, on one timeline for the order and the patient.

The routine items are handled by the system. What reaches a person is the exception, with the history attached.

## A way to start this week

Pick one order from last week that took longer than it should have. Write down every channel it touched, who looked at it, and how long it waited between them. The waits are almost always between channels, not inside them.
