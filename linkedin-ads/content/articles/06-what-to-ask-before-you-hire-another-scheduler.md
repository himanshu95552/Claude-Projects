# What to ask before you hire another scheduler

*For imaging center owners and managers*

When the phones are ringing and the fax tray is full, the obvious answer is another person at the desk. Sometimes it's the right one. But a new hire absorbs the problem without fixing it, and the next hire is waiting behind it.

Before you post the job, it's worth asking where the hours actually go. Here are the questions we'd start with. They're grouped by where in the order the work piles up.

## Where orders disappear

- Can you name the referring doctors who sent you fewer orders this quarter than last, without building a spreadsheet?
- Do you know which referring offices send their imaging somewhere else?
- Does a person read, match and key in every faxed order?
- When an order is missing information, does someone have to call or fax the office back?
- Can you see how many orders are waiting to be entered, and how long the oldest has waited?

## Where visits stall

- Does booking a patient mean someone calls them, maybe more than once?
- Can a patient book or move an appointment outside office hours?
- Can a slot be booked before the authorization is ready?
- When a patient doesn't show, does someone contact them to rebook, and is the slot offered to anyone waiting?
- Do patients fill out forms on paper at the front desk?

## Where money and goodwill slip

- Is coverage checked before the day of the exam, including who is primary?
- Does every exam that needs a prior authorization have one before the patient arrives?
- Does the patient know what they will owe before the visit?
- When a claim is denied, can you tell which step upstream caused it?
- Does the signed report reach the referring office without a phone call asking for it?

## How to use the list

Answer each one yes, no or not sure. A "no" or "not sure" marks a place worth tracing. You don't need a score. You need the two or three answers that cost you the most.

Then pick one order that touched those steps and follow it from the referral to the payment. Count how many times it waited on a person.

If most of the waiting is on steps that are the same every time, such as reading a fax, checking coverage or texting a patient, then the work is routine, and routine work is the kind software can take. If the waiting is on judgment calls, such as a payer exception or a patient with unusual needs, then another person at the desk may be exactly what you need.

The question isn't whether to hire. It's what you'd have the new person do.
