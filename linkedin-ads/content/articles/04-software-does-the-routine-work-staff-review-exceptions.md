# Software does the routine work. Your staff review the exceptions. What that actually means.

*For radiologist-owners and anyone wary of "AI" claims*

Every vendor says AI now. If you run an imaging center, you've probably learned to hear that as noise, and you're right to. The useful questions are plain ones: what does it do by itself, what does it hand to a person, and who is responsible when it's wrong?

Here is how we answer them for Gravity.

## What it does not do

It doesn't read the scan. It doesn't write the report. Gravity doesn't touch the clinical read at all. Your radiologists keep doing the work only they can do.

What it handles is the work around the scan: getting the order in, booking the patient, settling coverage and authorization, collecting what the patient owes, and making sure the signed report gets back to the person who ordered it. We say it plainly because it's the question radiologist-owners ask first: we don't read the scan, we make sure it gets back.

## What it does by itself

Routine, repeatable work. Reading a fax and filing it to the right patient. Checking benefits. Texting a patient a link to open slots. Submitting an authorization on a payer portal. Sending a signed report to the referring office, and again if it's amended.

## What it hands to a person

Anything it can't resolve. If a fax doesn't match one patient, one provider and one exam, a person sees a draft to confirm. If an authorization request needs a second look, it goes to the specialist. If an AI voice agent can't finish a booking call, a person picks it up with the full history attached. Those exceptions arrive in one shared list, assigned by capacity and skill.

## How much it's trusted is your decision

This is the part that matters most. In Gravity, each center chooses how much a person reviews. For order intake, some centers have staff review every order. Others let routine orders finish automatically and review only the unusual ones. For authorizations, a record can be set to review every run, finish on its own, or be turned off.

You can start with everything reviewed and loosen it as you see the work. You can also see what it did. Changes, communications and automated runs are logged, and each order has a timeline showing what happened and when.

## Five questions to ask any vendor claiming AI

1. What does it do without a person touching it?
2. What does it send to a person, and how does that person see it?
3. Can I turn the automation down, per task, if I don't trust it yet?
4. Can I see exactly what it did on one order, step by step?
5. What happens when it's wrong?

A good answer to the fifth is specific. If the answer is vague, treat the product as unproven.

## What this looks like in practice

At Iowa Radiology, two prior authorization specialists handle all of the practice's high-tech imaging. After Gravity went live, it now handles at least 80 percent of their authorizations. The specialists still work a handful of payers by hand, and no one was added to the team. By the executive director's account, the dark cloud over the team lifted.

That's the point of the whole approach. Agents work the order. People work the exceptions.
