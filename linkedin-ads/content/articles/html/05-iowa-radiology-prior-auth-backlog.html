How Iowa Radiology cleared a prior authorization backlog without adding staff

Customer story. Facts and quotes are from Iowa Radiology's published account on alphanodus.com.

Iowa Radiology was founded in 2001 and runs four outpatient imaging clinics in Iowa, offering MRI, CT, PET/CT, ultrasound, women's imaging and X-ray. The American College of Radiology has designated it a Breast Imaging Center of Excellence.

The problem

In December 2024, the practice moved to a new radiology information system. The connection to the prior authorization software it had used for years did not carry over.

The plan was to handle prior authorization inside the new RIS. That took months to arrive and didn't work, and the backlog grew to several months deep. Two prior authorization specialists handle all of the practice's high-tech imaging. Without automation, they were calling payers and logging into payer portals one order at a time. At any given moment, the practice was at least two weeks behind.

Executive Director Mendy Christensen put it plainly: "It became less of a discussion and more of an emergency. We have to change something and do it now."

What they did

The practice's IT team built the interfaces quickly. Once those were in place, Iowa Radiology was ready to go live within days. The Gravity team worked alongside the two specialists and their clinic supervisor, including overnight and over a weekend, until the backlog was under control.

Chief Information Officer Michael Riesberg said the implementation "saved our practice significant time and money," and that it took days, not the months a project like this usually takes.

What changed

On day one, the team counted about 430 orders waiting on two lists. By day three or four, that backlog was gone. Within about four weeks, the practice had cleared the full backlog and was keeping pace with new incoming orders.

Today, on a typical day, 40 to 50 orders are waiting on authorization and moving through as part of normal work. Iowa Radiology has been fully live since the end of June 2025. Gravity handles at least 80 percent of its authorizations, and the specialists still work a handful of payers by hand, where the process takes about the same time either way.

The practice added no staff. Before Gravity, it had been preparing to ask for budget to hire more prior authorization people. The specialists no longer see the need.

What it meant for patients

With no orders stuck waiting on authorization, patients reach their appointments sooner. Three of the four clinics are now at capacity in every modality. And when the practice books two weeks out, it knows the demand is real and not caused by authorizations it can't get.

Referring physicians noticed. Riesberg said, "The speed at which we can provide service to our customers is that brand loyalty that's almost intangible."

Christensen described the effect on the team: "We are totally caught up. We have no backlog."

What to take from it

The problem wasn't the specialists. It was a system change that cut a connection nobody had planned to replace. When the work around the order is automated, the people who know the payers can spend their time on the cases that need them.
