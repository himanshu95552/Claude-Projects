Where an imaging order actually gets lost

For imaging center owners and managers

Most imaging centers can tell you how many exams they did last month. Fewer can tell you how many orders never reached them, how many reached them and never got scanned, or how many were scanned and never fully paid. Those orders don't show up in any report, because nothing was recorded. That is what makes them expensive.

Follow one order from start to finish and you'll find three places it can go missing.

Before it arrives

A referring office decides where to send a patient. Sometimes that decision happens quietly. A physician who used to send you MRIs starts sending them somewhere else, and nobody at your center notices, because there was no event to notice. No cancelled appointment, no phone call. Just an order that didn't come.

Even when the order is coming to you, it often arrives as a fax. It sits in a tray or a queue until someone reads it, finds the patient, picks the exam and keys it in. If a detail is missing, someone calls the referring office, or sends a cover sheet back and waits for the reply.

Before it's scanned

An order that exists is not yet an exam that happens. Someone has to reach the patient, find a slot that fits the device and the exam length, confirm coverage, and for many exams get a prior authorization. Each of those steps usually means a person with a phone, a payer portal, or both. If one of them stalls, the slot goes empty or the patient gets rescheduled.

Early in our work, sensors in imaging centers showed scanners in use about 60 to 65 percent of the time while patients were still waiting for appointments. The machines weren't the limit. The work around them was.

After the exam

The scan is done and the report is signed, and the last leak is the quietest. A claim goes out with the wrong payer, or with no authorization on file, or with an estimate the patient never saw. The denial arrives weeks later, in a queue, for a visit nobody remembers well. Fixing it means digging up information that was easy to get when the patient booked.

Why you can't see it

Each leak lives in a different system. The CRM knows the referring offices. The RIS knows orders and schedules. The billing system knows claims. People carry the order from one to the next, and the places they carry it are where things get dropped. No single report covers the whole path, so every team can honestly say it's doing fine.

A way to look

You don't need new software to see this. Pick one order from last week that took longer than it should have. Write down every step from the referral to the payment, who touched it, and how long it waited. Mark every place it waited on a person, a fax or a phone call.

You will probably find a few stalls. Those are where your capacity and your cash are going.
