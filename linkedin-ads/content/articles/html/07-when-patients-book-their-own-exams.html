What happens when patients book their own exams

Customer story. Facts and quotes are from Downtown Radiology's published account on alphanodus.com.

Until October 2025, a person on Downtown Radiology's booking team booked every exam. When an order arrived, staff had to reach the patient and agree on a time.

Then the center let patients book their own exams from a text message. Over the next twelve months, the share of bookings made by patients rose from 19.7 percent to 43.8 percent. Patients didn't need an app, a password or a call to the front desk.

How it works

Once an order exists, the patient receives a text with a secure link to open slots. They open it without an app or a password, choose a location and a time, and the booking follows the same rules the booking team uses. Patients who don't book themselves are booked by the team. Patients can move an appointment from the same link when plans change.

The center's CEO, Arshia Bivadi, attested to the figures from the center's own data in Gravity. Only totals were used. No patient records left the system.

The numbers

  • 56.5 percent of patients who receive the text book themselves. The team books the rest.
  • 83.5 percent of those patients booked within a day of the order arriving.
  • The median time to book was 1.8 hours.
  • 38.1 percent booked within an hour.
  • 16.5 percent booked in the evening, overnight or at the weekend, when the front desk was closed.
  • 94.2 percent of the exams patients booked themselves were completed.

That last figure covers only patients who chose to book themselves. It isn't a comparison with exams the team booked, and the center doesn't claim it is.

What the CEO said

Bivadi on timing: "They book when it suits them. One in six books in the evening or at the weekend, when nobody could have picked up the phone."

On changing plans: "When plans change, they move the appointment from the same link: more than 5,000 did last year."

On where to start: "Start with the exam you book most often and let patients book it from a text."

And on the overall result: "Patients are ready to do this."

What it meant for the team

Routine bookings now arrive already made. Staff can spend their time on patients who need a person, including complex exams, callers and bookings that need review.

What to take from it

Self-booking didn't remove the booking team. It removed the part of their day spent on routine calls, so the team could spend more of it on the exceptions. Notice also how quickly patients book: more than eight in ten within a day, and nearly four in ten within the first hour. A slot that waits for a callback is often a slot that fills later than it could.
