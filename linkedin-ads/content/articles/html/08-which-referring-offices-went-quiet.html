Which referring offices went quiet? A way to find out this week

For imaging center owners and radiologist-owners

A referring office rarely tells you it has stopped sending patients. There is no cancellation and no complaint. The orders just get thinner, and because nothing happened, nothing shows up on a report.

You can check this yourself in an afternoon, with the data you already have.

Step 1. Pull four quarters of orders by referring provider

Ask your RIS or billing system for completed exams grouped by the ordering provider, for the last four quarters. You want a table with one row per provider and one column per quarter.

Step 2. Fix the names before you read the numbers

The same physician often appears twice: with and without a middle initial, under an old practice, or with a typo. That splits one referrer's volume into two small ones, and a real drop hides inside the noise. Match on the provider's NPI number where you have it, and merge duplicates before you compare anything.

Step 3. Compare the quarters

List your top 25 referring providers by volume. For each, compare the most recent quarter with the one before it, and with the same quarter a year ago. The year-ago comparison matters because many practices have seasons of their own.

Decide for yourself what size of drop is worth a call. Any rule you pick is fine, as long as you write it down and apply it the same way every quarter.

Step 4. Pick up the phone

Email is easy to ignore. Call the office and ask a plain question: what would make it easier to send you patients? The answers are usually practical. They want to see where an order stands without calling, they want the report back quickly, or they need a faster answer when something is missing from an order.

Step 5. Look at who is not on the list

The harder question is about physicians who never sent you anything, or who send to the center down the road. A list of every provider in your market who can order imaging, with who sends to you and who sends elsewhere, is more work to build by hand. It is the part most centers skip.
