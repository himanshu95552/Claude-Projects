# Organic LinkedIn posts (drafts)

Voice: plain, specific, no hype. Written for a company page or a named founder/leader. Links go in the first comment. Facts come from the Alpha Nodus site and customer pages; items marked (approval) need the customer's sign-off.

**1. The fax**
Somewhere in your imaging center there's a tray, a queue or a shared inbox with faxed orders in it.

Each one needs someone to read it, find the patient, pick the exam and key it in. If a detail is missing, someone calls the referring office and waits.

Nothing is broken. It's just that every order passes through a person before anything else happens.

Where does yours sit the longest?

**2. The order that never came**
A referring physician stops sending you MRIs. No cancellation, no call. Just fewer orders.

Nobody at your center notices, because nothing happened. That's the leak that never shows up in a report.

**3. Denials come late**
A denial arrives weeks after the visit, in a queue, for a patient nobody remembers well.

Most of what causes it was knowable when they booked: the right payer, whether an authorization was needed, what the patient would owe.

Five checks at booking beat fifty calls later.

**4. 60 to 65 percent**
Early on, sensors in imaging centers showed scanners in use about 60 to 65 percent of the time, while patients were still waiting for appointments.

The machine wasn't the limit. The work around it was.

**5. We don't read the scan**
A question radiologist-owners ask first: does the software touch the read?

No. Gravity doesn't read the scan or write the report. It handles the work around it, and makes sure the signed report gets back to the person who ordered it.

**6. Turn it down**
One test for any "AI" product: can you turn the automation down per task?

In Gravity, each center decides how much a person reviews. Start with everything reviewed. Loosen it as you see the work.
