# Vegetarian food for an Indian lacto-vegetarian honeymoon couple (23-31 Jan 2027): Thailand Andaman coast, Sri Lanka, Maldives local islands, Andaman Islands, Vietnam

Research notes, compiled 17 Nov 2026. Method: web search across travel blogs (incl. Indian-audience blogs), TripAdvisor/Wanderlog/Restaurant Guru/HappyCow listings and operator pages. **Limitation:** direct page fetches of HappyCow, thaiholidayguide.com, srilankaholidayguides.com and thecrazytravellers.com failed (host not reachable from this research session), so the findings below rest on search-result extracts of those pages, not on full-page reads. No Reddit threads came up in any search, so "how Indians rate it" rests on Indian travel blogs, TripAdvisor reviews and Indian tour-operator content. Ratings, opening status and prices change, so each named restaurant should be checked on Google Maps in January 2027.

**Season note (inference, applies to all five):** late January is peak dry season for the Thai Andaman coast, Sri Lanka's south and west coasts, the Maldives and the Andamans. Seasonal beach restaurants should be open. Vietnamese Lunar New Year (Tết) 2027 falls on 6 Feb 2027 (general knowledge, not sourced here), so 23-31 Jan is the busy run-up to Tết. Most restaurants should be open, but family-run places may start closing for Tết in the last days of the trip.

## 1. Thailand Andaman coast (Phuket, Krabi/Ao Nang/Railay, Koh Phi Phi, Koh Lanta, Koh Yao Noi)

### Takeaway
Phuket (especially Patong) is the easiest of all the beach areas on this list for Indian vegetarians. It has several pure-veg and Jain Indian restaurants plus Old Town "jay" (vegan) shops. Ao Nang is nearly as good (Govinda's plus many North Indian places). Koh Lanta is fair. Koh Phi Phi and Koh Yao Noi are thin and need planning. The main hidden-ingredient risks are fish sauce (nam pla), oyster sauce and shrimp paste (kapi) in curry pastes. Ask for "jay" food, or say clearly "no fish sauce".
**Veg-friendliness score: 4/5 overall** (Patong/Phuket 4.5, Ao Nang 4, Koh Lanta 3.5, Koh Phi Phi 3, Koh Yao Noi 2.5).

**Hidden ingredients and Thai phrases**
- **Jay (เจ)** = strictly vegan and also free of garlic, onion and other pungent vegetables. Jay shops fly a **yellow flag**. Everyone understands "jay". **Mangsawirat (มังสวิรัติ)** = vegetarian, usually allows egg, and may still contain fish sauce, shrimp paste or oyster sauce unless you specify — [Messy Vegan Cook](https://messyvegancook.com/traveling-vegan-in-thailand/amp/); [Learn Thai from a White Guy](https://learnthaifromawhiteguy.com/a-vegan-in-thailand); [Muay Ying: vegan/veg/Jain in Thailand](https://muay-ying.com/eating-vegan-vegetarian-jain-in-thailand)
- Jay food is no-onion/no-garlic, which makes it naturally close to **Jain-friendly** (though jay food may contain root vegetables such as potato or carrot) — [Muay Ying](https://muay-ying.com/eating-vegan-vegetarian-jain-in-thailand); [Lion Brand](https://lionbrand.com.au/jay-is-it-someones-name-a-type-of-food-or-is-it-something-else/)
- Fish sauce = nam pla; "mai sai nam pla" = don't add fish sauce. A blogger warns that an ordinary Thai restaurant asked to leave out nam pla may add another animal-based seasoning instead, so state the request clearly. Vegetarian oyster sauce is usually mushroom-based ("mushroom stir-fry sauce"). Vegan shrimp paste is sold in jay shops — [Yumsome](https://www.yumsome.com/mai-nam-pla/); [Messy Vegan Cook alternatives](https://www.messyvegancook.com/vegan-alternatives-to-fish-sauce-shrimp-paste-and-oyster-sauce-in-thai-cuisine/)

### Inferences
- Useful scripts (standard Thai, built from the sourced terms): "Gin jay" (I eat jay/vegan); "Mai sai nam pla, mai sai kapi, mai sai nam man hoi" (no fish sauce, no shrimp paste, no oyster sauce); "Mai sai khai" (no egg). Thai curry pastes (red, green, massaman) almost always contain shrimp paste, so a curry at an ordinary Thai restaurant is unsafe unless the place is jay or makes its own veg paste.
- For a lacto-veg couple, "jay" food is safest but has no dairy. Indian restaurants supply paneer and dahi.
- Patong has the densest pure-veg/Jain Indian cluster on this whole list outside India. Base a Phuket stay in Patong/Kata/Karon or plan Old Town lunches.
- Resort breakfasts in Phuket/Krabi typically include Western buffet items (bread, fruit, cereal, eggs) and are usually easy for lacto-vegetarians. Most mid/upscale hotels catering to Indian travellers add some Indian dishes. This is an inference: no source was found that specifically covers Andaman-coast resort breakfasts.

### Gaps
- No 2024-2026 confirmation that Full Moon Restaurant (Phi Phi) is still open, and no named Indian restaurant on Phi Phi from a recent source.
- No price figures in THB for the Phuket/Krabi Indian restaurants. Listings describe them as budget to mid-range.
- No Reddit or forum threads from Indian travellers were found.

## 2. Sri Lanka (Negombo, Kandy, Ella, Mirissa, Weligama, Galle, Unawatuna, Bentota, Tangalle)

### Takeaway
Sri Lankan rice-and-curry is naturally full of vegetable curries and dhal, so vegetarians eat well everywhere. The big trap is **Maldive fish** (dried, cured tuna flakes), which appears in pol sambol, lunu miris, many vegetable curries and even some dhal. Ask for each dish "without Maldive fish". Pure-veg Indian restaurants are concentrated in Colombo and Kandy. Beach towns have veg-friendly cafés but few pure-veg Indian kitchens.
**Veg-friendliness score: 4/5** (Kandy 4.5, Ella 4.5, Mirissa/Unawatuna 4, Galle/Weligama/Bentota 3.5, Negombo/Tangalle 3, with no specific sources found for those two).

- Maldive fish is *optional* in the basic sambol recipe and in lunu miris (the chilli relish eaten with kiribath/milk rice), so plain versions exist if requested — [Lonely Planet](https://www.lonelyplanet.com/articles/what-to-eat-and-drink-in-sri-lanka); [Bluelanka Tours](https://www.bluelankatours.com/blog/vegetarian-restaurants-sri-lanka)
- A Sri Lankan rice-and-curry spread "typically includes vegetable, lentil (dhal) and sometimes fish or meat" curries — [Lankawebsites](https://lankawebsites.com/blog/food-culture-local-life/vegetarian-and-vegan-travel-in-sri-lanka-how-easy-is-it)

### Inferences
- Sri Lankan breakfasts (string hoppers, pittu, kiribath, roti with dhal) are naturally vegetarian if served with dhal or coconut gravy instead of fish curry, and if the sambol/lunu miris is made without Maldive fish. Ask the guesthouse the night before. Egg hoppers are common, so ask for plain hoppers.
- In a homestay or guesthouse, the simplest instruction is: no meat, no fish, no Maldive fish, no egg in any curry. Family cooks usually adapt well, because Buddhist/Hindu vegetarian cooking is familiar locally.
- Tamil-run "vegetarian hotels" (cafés) are common in Kandy and Colombo, less so in the deep south.

### Gaps
- No specific pure-veg or Indian restaurant found for **Negombo** or **Tangalle**. Expect to rely on hotel kitchens and mixed menus there.

## 3. Maldives local islands (Maafushi, Thoddoo, Dhigurah, Ukulhas)

### Takeaway
Maafushi is the only one of the four with a real choice of Indian and veg-friendly restaurants. On Thoddoo, Dhigurah and Ukulhas most meals are eaten at the guesthouse or one of a handful of seafood-led cafés. Basic veg curries, rice, roshi (chapati), pasta, pizza and fruit (Thoddoo is a farming island) are available. Guesthouses cook vegetarian, including Indian-style, **when told in advance**, but this depends on the individual kitchen rather than being guaranteed. Maldivian food is fish-based (tuna in mas huni, garudhiya fish broth, tuna in "short eats" snacks), so assume tuna unless told otherwise.
**Veg-friendliness score: 3/5 overall** (Maafushi 3.5; Ukulhas 3; Thoddoo and Dhigurah 2.5).

### Cited Findings
- All four islands can provide basic vegetarian options such as vegetable curries and rice. Dhigurah and Maafushi have a somewhat wider choice of cafés. Strict vegans should message guesthouses in advance — [Maldives Traveler](https://www.maldivestraveler.org/maafushi-vs-dhigurah-vs-thoddoo-which-island-wins/)
- **Maafushi:** a couple reviewing Maafushi Village on TripAdvisor said staff cooked Indian vegetarian meals for them, and the food was good (older review) — [TripAdvisor](https://www.tripadvisor.com/ShowUserReviews-g2553632-d8812672-r562147061-Maafushi_Village-Maafushi_Island.html)
- **Maafushi:** at Kaani Grand Seaview a vegetarian guest said the hotel prepared veg dishes only when asked in advance. Its Aveli restaurant serves veg and non-veg Indian dishes, and a Wanderlog reviewer praises Aveli for Indian food and veg value — [TripAdvisor Kaani Grand Seaview 2026](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Hotel_Review-g2553632-d13076132-Reviews-Kaani_Grand_Seaview-Maafushi_Island.html); [Wanderlog Maafushi](https://wanderlog.com/list/geoCategory/198547/where-to-eat-best-restaurants-in-maafushi-island)
- **Maafushi:** Masala Street is a good veg option (roti, biryani), though one reviewer reported slow service and unavailable items. Semili's is veg-friendly with vegan options. Sapphire Café has Indian dishes and veg options — [Wanderlog Masala Street](https://wanderlog.com/place/details/7632161/masala-street); [TripAdvisor Semili's](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Restaurant_Review-g2553632-d24069542-Reviews-Semili_s_Maafushi-Maafushi_Island.html); [Life of Doing](https://lifeofdoing.com/where-to-eat-maafushi-maldives/)
- A list of "6 best Indian restaurants in Maafushi" exists from an Indian tour operator — [Skylark Tours](https://skylarktours.com/best-indian-restaurants-in-maafushi-island-maldives/)
- **Thoddoo:** Astro Café & Restaurant is veg-friendly (curries, salads from local produce) but mostly seafood. Local cafés mainly serve grilled fish, juices and Maldivian short eats. Thoddoo has fewer places to eat, and much of its food is farm produce — [TripAdvisor Astro](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Restaurant_Review-g6487172-d25081386-Reviews-Astro_Cafe_Restaurant-Thoddoo.html); [Relax Residence/Astro](https://relaxresidencemaldives.com/restaurant/); [Madi Maldives comparison](https://madimaldives.com/ukulhas-guide/quiet-local-islands-maldives-compared/)
- **Dhigurah:** dining is limited (Shell Restaurant, Café Dune) and most meals are served at hotels. Bliss Dhigurah's Hermit's restaurant serves vegetarian plates alongside reef fish and wood-fired pizza — [Bliss Dhigurah comparison](https://bliss.mv/en/dhigurah-vs-maafushi-ukulhas-dhangethi/); [Bliss](https://bliss.mv/en/)
- **Ukulhas:** around a dozen restaurants. Vegetarian, lactose-free and halal options are available. Paguro Café (Paguro Beach Inn) lists vegetarian, and Celeste Restaurant has a vegan curry — [Maldives Magazine Ukulhas](https://maldives-magazine.com/top-10/10-best-ukulhas-restaurants.htm); [Maldives Magazine Ukulhas vs Dhigurah](https://maldives-magazine.com/budget/ukulhas-vs-dhigurah.htm)

### Inferences
- **Do guesthouses reliably provide veg meals if pre-requested?** Mostly yes, by anecdote. Many Maldivian guesthouse cooks are Bangladeshi, Indian or Sri Lankan and can make dal, veg curry, chapati and rice. Evidence comes from a few reviews, not a systematic source. Book half- or full-board only after a written confirmation of Indian vegetarian meals, "no fish, no tuna flakes, no fish stock, no egg in curries".
- Dairy (paneer, dahi) is scarce on small islands because everything is shipped in. Expect few paneer dishes outside Maafushi.
- Local islands are alcohol-free, which is not relevant to food but matters for a honeymoon. Resort-island day trips or sandbanks usually include a buffet with veg items.
- For a strongly veg-focused couple, Maafushi (most choice, closest to Malé) is the safest food base. Thoddoo's fruit farms (watermelon, papaya) add fresh produce.

### Gaps
- No 2024-26 Reddit or Indian-blog first-hand accounts specific to Thoddoo, Dhigurah or Ukulhas veg food.
- No source quantified how many guesthouses offer Indian meals. Prices not captured.

## 4. Andaman Islands, India (Port Blair, Havelock/Swaraj Dweep, Neil/Shaheed Dweep)

### Takeaway
As expected for India, vegetarian food is easy: there are dedicated pure-veg restaurants on all three islands, South and North Indian thalis, and dosa/idli breakfasts. Resorts serve Indian veg buffets. Seafood dominates the beach-café scene on Havelock, and Jain food is limited on Havelock and harder still on Neil, so arrange it in advance.
**Veg-friendliness score: 4.5/5** (Port Blair 4.5, Havelock 4.5, Neil 3.5-4).

### Cited Findings
- **Port Blair:** Annapurna, Icy Spicy and Gagan are named pure-veg restaurants, and Jain meals can usually be arranged if requested in advance. Justdial lists The Grains (Aberdeen) and Island Arcade (Junglighat) as pure veg — [The Crazy Travellers (via search)](https://thecrazytravellers.com/pure-veg-and-jain-restaurants-in-andaman/); [Andaman Bliss](https://andamanbliss.com/blogs/best-vegetarian-restaurants-in-andaman-islands-top-veg-foods); [TripXL Port Blair veg](https://tripxl.com/blog/veg-restaurants-in-port-blair/)
- **Havelock:** Shakahaar Pure Veg (Govind Nagar) has 4.0 from about 601 Justdial ratings and is widely described as the go-to for vegetarians, serving only veg food (gobi Manchurian, roti-curry). Vegan options are rated "poor" — [Justdial](https://www.justdial.com/Port-Blair/Shakahaar-Pure-Veg-Restaurant-Havelock/9999P3192-3192-190418213426-B9H9_BZDET); [Wanderlog](https://wanderlog.com/place/details/2194561/shakahaar-pure-veg-restaurantshakahaari); [Spinach.guide](https://spinach.guide/venues/shakahaar-pure-veg-178700/)
- **Havelock:** other pure-veg names: Flavours Pure Veg Resto, Biswas Restaurant, Go Green Pure Veg, Govind's Pure Veg Café, The Veg Corner. ### Inferences
- No hidden-ingredient problem of the Southeast Asian kind: "veg" means veg in India. The main concern is shared kitchens and fryers at seafood shacks. Pure-veg restaurants avoid that.
- Hindi works everywhere. "Pure veg", "bina pyaaz-lehsun" (without onion and garlic) and "Jain khana" are understood.
- Resort breakfasts are standard Indian buffets (poha, idli, paratha, eggs on the side) and are unproblematic.
- Indian travel blogs and Justdial consistently rate the Andamans as easy for vegetarians. It is the least risky option for veg food, though the least "exotic".

## 5. Vietnam (Hanoi Old Quarter, Ha Long Bay cruises, Hoi An)

### Takeaway
Vietnam has a strong Buddhist "chay" (vegetarian) restaurant culture, and Hanoi and Hoi An both have many chay restaurants plus several Indian restaurants, including pure-veg/Jain ones (Ram Krishna in Hanoi, Rasoi in Hoi An). The hidden-ingredient risk is fish sauce (nước mắm) in nearly everything outside chay restaurants, including dipping sauces and broths. Ha Long cruises cater to Indian vegetarians and many advertise Jain meals, but only with written advance notice, and the evidence is mostly operator marketing.
**Veg-friendliness score: 4/5 overall** (Hanoi 4.5, Hoi An 4.5, Ha Long cruise 3.5 if pre-arranged, 2 if not).

### Cited Findings
**Phrases and hidden ingredients**
- Say both halves: **"Tôi ăn chay, không nước mắm"** (I eat vegetarian, no fish sauce). "Tôi ăn chay" alone may be heard as a religious statement and fish sauce may still be added. Ask about egg with "có trứng không?" Vegan = "thuần chay". Fallback: "Không thịt, cá, hoặc nước mắm" (no meat, fish or fish sauce) — [Jungle Boss Tours](https://junglebosstours.com/explorer/tourism-blog/vegan-travel-vietnam)
- Add "chay" to dish names (phở chay, bánh mì chay). Look for "quán chay"/"cơm chay" on Google Maps and near pagodas. Outside veg restaurants, fish sauce is likely — [Vietnam.travel (official)](https://vietnam.travel/things-to-do/vegetarian-guide-vietnam); [My Vegan Travels](https://myvegantravels.com/destinations/asia/vietnam/vegan-vietnamese-phrases/)
- Chay is Buddhist and usually means no egg or dairy. Places that don't advertise "thuần chay" may not distinguish vegan from vegetarian — [Jungle Boss Tours](https://junglebosstours.com/explorer/tourism-blog/vegan-travel-vietnam); [HappyCow Vietnam](https://www.happycow.net/asia/vietnam/)
- Indian-vegetarian guides to Vietnam (2024/25) exist from Indian bloggers — [Findyouradventure.in](https://findyouradventure.in/guide-to-indian-vegetarian-food-in-vietnam-in-2024-2025/); [CurlyGirlyTravely (Apr 2024)](https://curlygirlytravely.in/2024/04/11/how-to-manage-vegetarian-or-vegan-food-for-vietnam-trip/)

### Inferences
- **Do Ha Long cruises reliably provide veg if requested in advance?** Yes for mid-range and luxury overnight cruises, provided the request is in writing at booking and re-confirmed 48 h before. Cruise menus default to seafood, so un-flagged guests risk a plate of steamed vegetables and rice. Ask specifically for no fish sauce in dipping sauces and no fish/chicken stock in soups.
- Breakfast at Hanoi and Hoi An hotels typically includes bread, eggs, fruit, phở and noodle stations. The phở broth is beef or chicken, so lacto-vegetarians should stick to bread, fruit, yoghurt and noodles stir-fried on request. This is a general inference, not sourced.
- Dairy is uncommon in Vietnamese food (lacto-veg protein comes from tofu), so Indian restaurants are where paneer and dahi are found.

### Gaps
- No independent (non-operator) 2025-26 reviews of veg/Jain meals on named Ha Long cruises.
