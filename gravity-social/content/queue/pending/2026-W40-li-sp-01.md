## Post

I needed a scan, and I couldn't get one for six weeks.

I'd spent twenty years in wireless semiconductors. In that world everything runs just-in-time. An idle machine is somebody's emergency.

Later I learned that most imaging centers have a third of their capacity going unused every day. The scanner I needed was probably sitting empty somewhere while I waited.

That made me angry enough to leave my day job.

We didn't know anything about imaging, so we asked a center to let us volunteer. We did every job in the building for a year: front desk, scheduling, intake, authorizations.

I hated most of it, and I understood all of it.

Here's what I understood. Most of the waiting isn't clinical. It's operational. The order is stuck in a fax tray, on a callback list, behind a payer portal.

Just-in-time exists in manufacturing, logistics, every industry you can name, except healthcare. Nobody should wait for care because of paperwork.

What's the longest you've waited for a scan that turned out to be a scheduling problem?
