## Post

The MRI is done. The approval never was. Weeks later the denial arrives, and nothing can fix it now.

Starting today, Surest plans (a UnitedHealthcare company) require prior authorization for outpatient CT, MRI and MRA, PET and nuclear cardiology. The imaging center has to confirm a coverage decision exists before the exam, not after. The notice is in the first comment.

Rules like this keep moving. Codes are added, plans change portals, some requirements are dropped. Most schedulers find out the same way: a rejected claim, long after the patient has gone home.

Here's the uncomfortable part. Most denials aren't billing mistakes. They're scheduling-stage mistakes that nobody catches until weeks later.

So Gravity settles the money questions at scheduling. It checks eligibility, determines whether authorization is required, works the payer portal and requests the progress notes from the referring office. Your authorization specialist answers the clinical questions. The patient arrives cleared.

Every exam you complete, you get paid for.
